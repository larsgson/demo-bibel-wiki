# Shepherd to King

## 04.01 Joshua is called

*Forty years later — a new leader, an old promise.*

After the Hebrews had walked in the desert for 40 years, God called a man called Joshua —
whose name means the same as the name of Jesus. It means “The Lord Saves.”
Joshua led the people down to the River Jordan
and sent men carrying the Ark of the Covenant through the river, ahead of the people.

## 04.02 Crossing the Jordan

*Another sea, another miraculous crossing.*

God parted the waters just as he had
when they went through the Red Sea after leaving Egypt,
and the people entered the land of Canaan
and settled there.

## 04.03 The age of judges and prophets

*The way was shown — but the people didn't follow.*

Over the years, they had judges and prophets to guide them.
Yet again the people failed to live the lives that were intended for them.
They did what was right in their own eyes and did not follow God and his ways.

## 04.04 The people demand a king

*The warning was given, but the decision doesn't change.*

When people were being guided by a good prophet called Samuel,
the people wanted a king like the nations around them had.
Samuel was upset and tried to warn them of the consequences of this —
that a king would take their wealth, their daughters, and more —
but the people insisted that they wanted a king.

## 04.05 Saul becomes king

*It started well — what changed?*

God told Samuel to give them a king.
So the Lord led Samuel to anoint a man called Saul.
Saul started out well
but began to turn away from God and do things his own way.

## 04.06 God looks at the heart

*The youngest was chosen — why?*

God then chose a new king.
He guided Samuel to the home of a shepherd called Jesse.
Jesse had many sons,
but God told him to take the youngest son.
God did not choose the tallest, strongest, or most handsome.
He chose the youngest, who was out tending the sheep.

## 04.07 David is anointed

*For now, he goes on tending the sheep.*

This young man, who was called David, was a good and courageous shepherd who trusted in the Lord.
Samuel anointed him as the future king.

## 04.08 Before the battle

*His brothers are on the battlefield — he's with the sheep.*

One day the enemies of the Hebrews gathered for war and came to challenge them.
David's brothers went up to prepare to fight,
but David had to stay to look after his father's sheep.

## 04.09 The giant's challenge

*No one dares step forward.*

The enemy had a champion who was about 3 metres tall, and strong.
He said: “Choose a man and have him come down to me.
If he is able to fight and kill me, we will become your subjects;
but if I overcome him and kill him, you will become our subjects and serve us.”
The Hebrews were terrified.
The king offered all sorts of rewards,
but no one was willing to fight, as they were afraid.

## 04.10 The taunting continues

*What does David overhear?*

Every day this man came out and mocked and challenged the Hebrews to produce someone willing to fight him.
One day this young shepherd boy was asked by his father to take food to his brothers
and check up on them.
When he arrived at the army camp, he heard the enemy come out with the challenge
and mock the Hebrews and God.

## 04.11 David speaks up

*Armor is offered — but it's not for him.*

David immediately said he would fight and was brought to King Saul.
David looked small and insignificant,
but he protested that he had killed a bear and a lion to protect his sheep.
He said the Lord would protect him from this enemy just as he had from the bear and lion.
The king agreed and offered David all his personal armour,
but it was too big and heavy.

## 04.12 In his own way

*A sling and some stones — nothing else.*

David took it off and said he would fight his own way and trust in God.
So David took his slingshot, went down to a stream, picked up 5 smooth stones,
and went out to fight the giant man.

## 04.13 Two warriors face to face

*One stone, one moment.*

The man mocked and said, “Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks and stones?”
and went to fight David.
David declared that he came in the name of the Lord Almighty,
put a stone in his slingshot,
and threw it at Goliath,
and the stone hit him in the middle of his forehead.
Goliath fell to the ground,
and David took Goliath's sword and killed him.

## 04.14 The enemy retreats

*Fear is defeated.*

The army of Goliath fled,
and the Hebrews pursued them.
David continued to follow the Lord and was very successful in his battles.

## 04.15 Saul's jealousy

*Success turns into danger.*

He served Saul, who became so jealous that he attempted to kill David,
and David had to flee.

## 04.16 Years as a fugitive

*Wandering, before becoming king.*

David took his family to a safe place
and then wandered in the desert with a band of followers,
and they lived as nomads for years.

## 04.17 David becomes king

*One era ends, another begins.*

Eventually King Saul died in battle with the enemy,
and David became king of Israel.

## 04.18 The promise of an eternal throne

*Trust in God — and one great promise.*

David went on to protect his people, often fighting in battles.
David continued to consult Samuel the prophet,
and although he sometimes failed,
he always returned to God,
and during his reign
God told him that a descendant of David would be king forever.

## 04.19 How will the promise be kept?

*Good kings, bad kings — but the plan continues.*

After David died, there followed generations of kings who ranged from OK to bad,
but mostly terrible.
So how is it that God would keep his promise to this — and all peoples?
