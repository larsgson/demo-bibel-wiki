# The Cross and the Empty Tomb

## 09.01 Waiting in dark times

*Israel awaits the Messiah — but how will he come?*

The people of Israel were waiting for the Messiah.
They were living in dark times.
With the influence of the Greeks, moral standards were low,
and the religious leaders were calling people back to God by imposing legalistic interpretations of the Torah.

## 09.02 Herod and Roman rule

*How deep did the hardship go?*

They were living under the brutal empire of Rome,
notorious for its cruelty, and
people were struggling with poverty under the weight of taxes owed to a government far away.
Their own representative, King Herod, was cruel and ambitious.
Surely, of all times, this was the time the Messiah should come to save them and bring peace.

## 09.03 An unexpected arrival

*A King — but in what form?*

And yes, he came! …but in a surprising way.
He was born into a humble family,
became a refugee in Egypt as an infant,
and was raised in a rural village.
Jesus had come into the world to take on our lives.

## 09.04 Baptized in our place

*He never once went wrong — so why?*

Though he had never done wrong,
he was baptized in order to take on all our wrongdoing,
all our broken relationships, all our regrets, all our negligence,
and he lived the perfect life for us.
He showed all people the love of God —

## 09.05 Love for the outcast

*Not the path of power — a different path.*

— the poor, the sick, the persecuted,
those rejected by the religious leaders.
He came with all power and performed miracles,
but he did not take the road of political power.

## 09.06 The Passover lamb

*He knew exactly why he had come.*

Jesus explained that he had come to die for them all.
He was to be the once-and-for-all Lamb of sacrifice.

## 09.07 The triumphal entry into Jerusalem

*In peace — but what lies ahead?*

He rode into Jerusalem on a donkey
to show that he was coming in peace.
He had fulfilled all the prophecies,
and now he submitted to being arrested.

## 09.08 The Passover meal

*An ancient feast — a new meaning.*

It was the time when the people were celebrating the Passover feast,
remembering how God had rescued them from slavery.
They would slaughter a lamb, eat its meat, and eat unleavened bread,
remembering what God had done for them as a people —
slaughtering the lamb, painting its blood on their doors —
which saved them from death,
recalling how, in their haste to escape slavery, they had eaten unleavened bread.

## 09.09 The Last Supper

*Bread and wine — what do they mean?*

Jesus ate this meal with his disciples, gave thanks, and then broke the bread, saying,
“This is my body, broken for you.”
He took the cup of wine and gave it to them, saying, “This is my blood, shed for you.”
He was telling them that he was about to become the once-and-for-all sacrifice
that would take away their sin and shame and lead them into freedom.

## 09.10 In the garden of Gethsemane

*Agony, betrayal, arrest — all in one night.*

After this meal, he went outside and walked to a small hill where he often went with his disciples.
There he wept and prayed, showing the full weight of human anguish he was suffering.
Then he got up and allowed himself to be arrested by the soldiers sent by the religious leaders.
He told the soldiers to let his friends go, and most of them fled.

## 09.11 Unjust trials

*False witnesses, justice mocked.*

Jesus allowed himself to be brought before the senior religious leaders of the day,
and went through sham trials, hearing accusations from false witnesses, facing a corrupted system of justice.

## 09.12 Before Pilate

*Innocent — but not released.*

The religious leaders were determined to get rid of Jesus, and since only the political rulers had authority to sentence someone to death, they brought him to the Roman governor, Pilate.
They also stirred up the crowd outside to call for Jesus' death.
Pilate knew Jesus was innocent and tried to release him.
But Pilate was afraid of the crowd,
so he had Jesus flogged, hoping to appease them.

## 09.13 The crowd's demand

*Pilate was afraid — and then what?*

But the religious leaders stirred the crowd to demand Jesus be crucified.
This was the most severe and cruel death, reserved for the worst criminals,
carried out outside the city walls.

## 09.14 Three hours of darkness

*A question no one could answer.*

All of this had been foretold by the prophets.
Jesus was nailed to the cross to face a slow, agonizing death.
As he hung there, darkness fell for three hours.
At the end of his terrible suffering, Jesus cried out the words of a well-known Messianic psalm:
“My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” —
bearing the depths of that anguish for us.

## 09.15 “It is finished”

*One word — and everything ends.*

Then Jesus cried out once more, “It is finished.”
He died.

## 09.16 The temple curtain tears

*From top to bottom — on its own.*

And at that moment the great curtain in the temple —
which separated the innermost holy place from the rest of the temple,
symbolizing God's holy presence set apart from sinful humanity — was torn from top to bottom.
Jesus died as the sacrificial lamb, offering his life in our place —
that is, as a sacrifice for our sins.
And so the temple curtain was torn from top to bottom, not from bottom to top —
meaning it was not torn by human hands.
This underlined that God had removed everything that separated his people from him.

## 09.17 Direct access is opened

*What was once impossible — now possible.*

So, through Jesus' death, we now have direct access to God,
and Jesus has made us righteous before him, and
we can come before him trusting in his love and forgiveness.

## 09.18 The earth shakes

*What did a soldier cry out?*

The earth shook.
People were seen coming out of their tombs, and
a Roman military commander, gasping, said,
“Surely he was the Son of God!”

## 09.19 No bones were broken

*An ancient word fulfilled once again.*

Soldiers came to break the legs of those hanging on crosses so the bodies could be taken down,
but when they came to Jesus,
he was already dead, so instead they pierced his side with a spear,
and blood and water flowed out — proof that he was already dead.
The prophets had said that none of his bones would be broken,
but that people would “look on the one they had pierced.”

## 09.20 Laid in a new tomb

*A stone, a seal, guards — why?*

A man came and
asked Pilate for permission to place Jesus' body in a private tomb that had never been used before.
Pilate agreed.
According to custom, the body was wrapped in grave clothes and placed in the tomb,
a massive stone was set against the entrance to seal it,
and at the religious leaders' request, Roman soldiers were posted at the entrance,
to make sure no one could steal the body and claim that Jesus had risen.
After the burial, Jesus' followers gathered in a locked room, afraid that they too might be arrested.
They must have been overwhelmed with despair, fear, and confusion!

## 09.21 The empty tomb

*What do the women find?*

But at dawn on the first day of the week, some of the women went to the tomb to anoint Jesus' body with oil.
A violent earthquake struck, and the stone was rolled away from the entrance to the tomb.
A man in dazzling white clothing said to the women,
“He has risen, just as he said.”

## 09.22 A false story spreads

*A lie, bought and paid for.*

Terrified for their lives, the guards went to the religious leaders to ask what to do.
After discussing it, they gave the guards a large sum of money to say that Jesus' disciples had come at night and stolen the body,
promising to smooth things over with the authorities
so the guards wouldn't get in trouble.
This rumor spread widely.
The women went and told the men what had happened.

## 09.23 Running to the tomb

*What's left, and what isn't?*

Peter and John ran to the tomb.
Peter went in and saw the empty grave clothes, and John followed,
and they believed!
They went back to the others, and later, as they were gathered in a room,
suddenly Jesus was standing there among them!
“Peace be with you,” he said.

## 09.24 “Peace be with you”

*What is shown to the doubtful?*

“Why are you troubled, and why do doubts arise in your hearts?
As he spoke, he showed them the wounds in his hands and his side.
He told them to touch him, and asked for something to eat, which he ate.
This convinced the disciples that he was truly there in the flesh,
not a ghost.”

## 09.25 Thomas's doubt

*“Unless I see, I won't believe” — is that really true?*

One of his followers, Thomas, was not there,
and when he was told that Jesus had appeared, he said,
“Unless I see the nail marks in his hands, put my finger where the nails were,
and put my hand into his side, I will not believe.”
Eight days later, Jesus appeared to Thomas and said, “Put your finger here. Look at my hands.
Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe.”
Then Thomas cried out, “My Lord and my God!”

## 09.26 Appearances and the Great Commission

*More than 500 people — and one final command.*

Jesus appeared more than 11 times, and on one occasion to 500 people.
He taught them from Scripture, explaining why he had to die and rise again.
He told them to go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and
teaching them to obey everything he had commanded.
He emphasized that he would be with them until the end of the age.

## 09.27 The ascension

*He leaves — but leaves a promise.*

Jesus also told them that he would go to prepare a place for them and would come back.
Finally, they watched Jesus disappear into the sky.
After he had gone, they went to wait in Jerusalem, just as Jesus had told them.
