# The Prophets

## 05.01 A life prepared for

*What did God plan for His people? Let's not forget.*

We have talked about how God rescued these people from slavery
and brought them into their land,
protected by God as part of his plan
to bring blessing to all nations.

## 05.02 Unfaithfulness

*The promise was there — but did life reflect it?*

This life they were given, this partnership with God under the covenant,
was meant to be lived out.
However, the people and their king were not living in faithfulness to the covenant.

## 05.03 Prophets are sent

*A warning — but also a call to return.*

God continued sending them prophets
as a warning about where this kind of life would lead.
These prophets were people who had gone through a serious relationship with God themselves
and, by God's command,
were given the task of speaking to Israel in his name.
Their task was to warn the people about the outcome of their actions
and to call them back to God.

## 05.04 The prophets' opposition

*A long list of injustice — written for whom?*

The prophets spoke up against: oppression,
when the rich and powerful grew wealthy
at the expense of the poor;
dishonest gain;
bloodshed;
degrading treatment of slaves
and those lower in society;
slavery and the selling of the poor;
taxing the harvest of the poor;
extravagant living by some
while their neighbors were in poverty;
people seizing more land for themselves;
withholding wages;
the persecution of orphans and widows
and the mistreatment of foreigners;
land injustice — seizing land from the poor;
greed;
and irresponsible, wasteful use of the land they had been given.

## 05.05 A broken covenant

*This isn't just criticism — it's God's own opposition.*

This prophetic protest was the protest of God himself.
Israel had broken the requirements of their covenant relationship with God.
The land had been allotted to the people on the basis of justice.

## 05.06 Land laws

*A system unlike Egypt's — for whose benefit?*

The Torah had specific rules for a jubilee system,
allowing land to be restored to the poor who had had to sell it because of economic hardship — restored every 7 years.
The Torah also gave women the right to inherit land,
as long as it stayed within their clan.
Unlike Egypt and the surrounding nations — where landowners held special ownership and ordinary people simply worked for wages —
the Torah required that land in Israel belong to ordinary people.
Each family had the right to own
and work its own land.

## 05.07 Mercy, not religious ritual

*What does God actually want?*

“God is not interested in religious ceremonies,” the prophets said.
“He wants justice and truth.
He wants us to act justly and love mercy.”
He warned the leaders that if they continued down this unjust path, it would lead them to a completely wrong direction and outcome.

## 05.08 Prophecy of exile

*Hardship lies ahead — but so does hope.*

If Israel continued worshiping false gods
and oppressing ordinary people,
God said he would use powerful kingdoms against them — Assyria and Babylon —
and hand them over to slavery.
But the prophets also spoke of hope.
After being taken into exile by Assyria, and later by Babylon,
Israel waited with hope for the fulfillment of God's promise.

## 05.09 The coming King

*Peace, justice — through Whom?*

The prophets emphasized the coming of the Messiah — a descendant of King David, promised by God —
who through his own faithful life would fulfill the promise God gave to Abraham on Mount Sinai,
and who would bring blessing to all nations.
He would be given the name “Immanuel,” meaning “God with us.”
He was spoken of as his people's Keeper,
who would rule forever from the mountain of Jerusalem —
a King who would bring justice to ordinary people and lead all nations.
His kingdom would draw everyone into his peace.
He would be a light to all nations.
He would conquer sin, die and rise again,
reign in Jerusalem, and gather every nation,
bringing blessing to them all.

## 05.10 Where and how

*Foretold precisely — but an unexpected ending.*

The prophets also spoke of how the King would come —
that he would be born in Bethlehem, that he would be taken to Egypt, that he would be called a Nazarene.
But what would surprise the people
was that his own people would reject him and kill him.
He would be put to death for his people's sins —
the Servant's death a sacrifice for the people's sin and stubbornness.

## 05.11 Resurrection is proclaimed

*The promise of renewal — for three relationships.*

Then, on the basis of the prophecy,
it would be proclaimed that he was raised,
and that he would make every nation righteous.
He brings us resurrection and renewal of life.
This is the promise of renewed relationship —
the renewal of the relationship between God and humanity,
between person and person,
and between person and animal.
