# Abraham: The Nomad Who Received God's Promise for Humanity

## 02.01 Humanity's growing wickedness

*The world was falling apart — and it broke God's heart.*

People began to increase in number and increasingly turned to evil,
and God was “grieved that he had made humans and His heart was filled with pain”
— such was the Lord's love for his people.

## 02.02 Noah and the ark

*One family. One boat. A single chance to start again.*

God still loved them.
When a huge flood was coming, he rescued a good man, Noah,
telling him to make a large boat for his family, his livestock, and wildlife.

## 02.03 The covenant after the flood

*A promise sealed in the sky — but people turn away again.*

After the flood, God made a covenant of love with Noah, symbolized by a rainbow.
God sent them out,
but sadly, once again, people continued to turn from God's ways.
However, God had promised his people that he would not destroy them again in this way.

## 02.04 Abram's calling

*One man, one calling — through him, blessing for the whole world.*

Many generations after the flood, the Lord spoke to a man called Abram from Ur
and told him to leave his home
and follow God to a new land.
God gave Abram a promise, saying that he would make a great nation from him,
and that all nations would be blessed through him.

## 02.05 The nomadic life begins

*Abram sets out with his household, toward an unknown land.*

So Abram set out, starting his nomadic life.
He took his wife and his family, his nephew and his family, his tents, his animals, his servants, and set off to follow God.

## 02.06 A vision: “Do not be afraid, Abram”

*A great promise — but a question weighs on Abram's heart.*

One day God appeared to Abram in a vision:
“Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your very great reward.”
Abram questioned God.
Despite God's promise about his descendants, Abram still did not have a son by his wife, and so had no heir.

## 02.07 Count the stars

*The stars in the sky — the measure of God's promise.*

Then God told him to look at the sky and count the stars — and told him his descendants would be like that in number.
Abram believed God.
God told him he would give them the land where he was located, and Abram asked, “How can I know?” Abram's faith was stretched!

## 02.08 The covenant ceremony

*The animals are split in two — why?*

God told him to go and bring a cow, a goat, and a ram, each three years old, and a dove and a pigeon.
He cut each animal in half and put them opposite each other,
but the birds he did not divide, just as God had told him.
Then, as nightfall came, Abram fell into a deep sleep.

## 02.09 A 400-year prophecy

*Hardship lies ahead — but freedom follows.*

God prophesied to him and told him that his descendants would be slaves in a foreign land for 400 years
and ill-treated,
but he would bring them out with great possessions, and he would punish this foreign nation.

## 02.10 Fire and smoke pass between

*Abram doesn't watch — but God himself acts.*

Then the Lord sealed his covenant.
Abram saw a smoking pot, and fire from a blazing torch — God's very presence — moved between the animals.
What does this mean?

## 02.11 A one-sided promise

*Not two parties, but One alone takes on the responsibility.*

In those days, if two leaders of nations were coming together to make an agreement, they would walk between animals that had been cut in half in this way to seal the promise.
The meaning is that they would take the penalty — like these animals who were killed — if they broke the agreement.
What is different here is that only one party walks between the carcasses.
God himself, represented by the flaming torch, passed between the animals.
God was confirming the covenant to Abraham, by guaranteeing that he would take the penalty even if the covenant were broken by man.

## 02.12 “I am the Strong God”

*A new title, a man facedown on the ground.*

When Abram was ninety-nine years old, God came and said to him,
“I am the Strong God, live entirely before me, faithfully!
I'll make a covenant between us, and I'll give you a huge family.”
Overwhelmed, Abram fell flat on his face.

## 02.13 A new name, a land forever

*Abram now carries a new name — and the promise grows wider.*

Then God said to him, “This is my covenant with you:
You will be the father of many nations.
Your name will no longer be Abram, but Abraham,
meaning that ‘I'm making you the father of many nations.’
I'll make you a father of fathers — I'll make nations from you, kings will be your descendants.
I'm establishing my covenant between me and you, a covenant that includes your descendants,
a covenant that goes on and on, a covenant that commits me to be your God and the God of your descendants.
And I'm giving you and your descendants this land where you are camping, this whole country of Canaan,
to own forever.”

## 02.14 Circumcision — sign of the covenant

*At 99 years old — and still, a son is promised.*

“And I'll be their God.”
God told Abraham to circumcise his whole household as a sign of the covenant,
of the promise God made with his people — a permanent sign of the promise.
Abraham was now 99, and his wife Sarah was 90.
God told him she would now give birth to a son, and he would make a large family from this son.

## 02.15 Ishmael's birth

*A human decision brings conflict — but the promise continues.*

Abraham had a child by his servant woman at Sarah's suggestion,
which caused conflict between them.
Yet God told Abraham to circumcise this son, Ishmael, too,
as his promise was for the “bought” as well as for the house-born — for the adopted as well as for those born to him.

## 02.16 An unconditional promise

*The key to this whole story — who answers for what?*

So, in summary, God made a lasting covenant with Abraham
that he would make a great nation of him,
and through his seed — his descendant — all nations would be blessed.
Unlike all other agreements of the day, this was a one-sided commitment in which God took all the responsibility on himself.
Here is the key to what the story is all about:
God bringing all people back into relationship with himself through a descendant of Abraham.

## 02.17 Isaac grows up

*Faith is tested — but at what cost?*

Abraham and Sarah had a son together,
and when the son was grown, Abraham believed God wanted him to sacrifice his son to God.
Abraham took his son and went out to make a sacrifice to God.

## 02.18 The journey to the sacrifice

*“God will see to it” — but see to what, for whom?*

When the son asked Abraham where the lamb was that was to be sacrificed,
Abraham said, “Son, God will see to it that there's a sheep for the burnt offering.”
And they kept on walking together. They arrived at the place to which God had directed him.
Abraham built an altar.

## 02.19 When the knife is raised

*A voice from heaven — and everything changes.*

He laid out the wood. Then he tied up Isaac and laid him on the wood.
Abraham reached out and took the knife to kill his son.
Just then an angel of God called out to him from heaven, “Abraham! Abraham!” “Yes, I'm listening.”
“Don't lay a hand on that boy! Don't touch him!” Abraham looked up. He saw a ram caught by its horns in the thicket.
Abraham took the ram and sacrificed it as a burnt offering instead of his son.

## 02.20 “The LORD Will Provide”

*One place, one name — a lesson that lasts forever.*

Abraham named that place God-Yireh (God-Sees-to-It).
That's where we get the saying, “On the mountain of God, he sees to it.”
God himself would provide the sacrificial lamb.

## 02.21 The real meaning of this

*Other gods demand a person — but this God provides one himself.*

What is the meaning of this?
The context is vital here.
At that time, among the nations around Abraham, child sacrifice was used to appease idols.
But God's people are called to be different from the nations around him.
The one true God reveals that he is not a god who demands horrific human sacrifice — rather, he will provide the lamb for the sacrifice.
He will see to it!
He is a God of love, and he still has his plan to bless the whole world!
It is God himself who will provide the sacrifice for our rescue, and the plan for us is his plan, not our works.

## 02.22 Noah's failure too

*Even after the flood — people go astray again.*

So let us summarize what we've looked at so far:
people turned away from God, breaking their commitment to God, to each other, and to the land.
Despite that, God in his mercy still guided them.
They were evil; a flood came,
and God saved the one who trusted in him — Noah.
When they came out of the boat onto dry land, having been saved,
he gave the same command he gave to Adam and Eve: go out and be fruitful —
go and take the presence of God into the whole world.
Yet even Noah and his family failed. Humanity kept going astray.

## 02.23 Through One Descendant — blessing for the whole world

*This is not our effort — this is His plan.*

God called Abraham to follow his leading as a nomad,
and from him he would make a nation,
and finally — through his seed, this word is singular, meaning his descendant —
bring blessing to the whole world.
This was not because we deserve it, but because it is his plan of love and his promise,
which he confirmed several times to Abraham.
It was not through human sacrifice or works, but God himself would make the sacrifice to bring about restoration for the whole world.
God's plan continues to be revealed, and this becomes clear as we follow the story of this family and nation,
right on to the descendant who was coming to save the world.
