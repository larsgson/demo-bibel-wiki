# Moses Leads Nomads Through the Desert

## 03.01 Slavery in Egypt begins

*The promise was given — but for now, only hardship.*

Just as the Lord had told Abraham would happen, his descendants ended up in slavery.
A Pharaoh of Egypt put them to hard labour and enslaved them.

## 03.02 Pharaoh's decree

*One mother, one basket, one desperate risk.*

He ordered the killing of all Hebrew boys under 2 years of age.
One Hebrew woman had a son and hid him for 3 months, but when she could no longer hide him,
she put him into a waterproof basket and hid him amongst the reeds on the River Nile.

## 03.03 A child pulled from the river

*Rescued in an extraordinary way — what will this infant be called?*

The princess of Egypt rescued the infant and brought him up in the palace. His name was Moses.

## 03.04 Moses flees

*One act of anger — and an entire life turns in a new direction.*

As a man, Moses went out and saw how his people were being treated,
and saw an Egyptian slave master beating a Hebrew.
In his anger he killed the Egyptian and had to flee from Egypt.
He travelled far to a foreign country.

## 03.05 A meeting at the well

*A kindness in a foreign land — where will it lead?*

and came to a place where some women were drawing water.
He saw shepherds come and chase the women away and take their water.
Moses rushed in to help the women, and gave water to their animals.
When the women went home and told their father what had happened,
he sent them out to invite Moses to eat with them.

## 03.06 A new family, a new life

*A fugitive — now a shepherd and a father.*

He took Moses in,
gave his daughter to him in marriage,
and Moses settled there as a sheep herder and had his own children.
One day, Moses took his father-in-law's sheep to the far side of the wilderness,
near Mount Sinai,

## 03.07 The burning bush

*A single flame — and holy ground.*

where he saw a bush burning but not being consumed.
He went over to look and heard the Lord call him by name.
He told Moses to take off his shoes, as this was holy ground.

## 03.08 God is listening

*The cry has been heard — but Moses isn't ready.*

The Lord told him that he had seen the misery of his people;
he had heard them crying out because of their slave drivers, and so had come to rescue them and take them to a new land,
and he was sending Moses to speak to Pharaoh on behalf of the people.
Moses had fled Egypt as a refugee, and now God is sending him back!
Moses protested: “Who am I that you send me?”

## 03.09 “Who am I?”

*One question, one answer — hard to grasp.*

and the Lord's answer was “I WILL BE WITH YOU.”
Continuing the protest, he asked who he should say God was.
God answered “I AM who I am.”
Moses protested that they might not believe him,

## 03.10 When the staff becomes a snake

*An answer to doubt — through signs.*

and so God said, “Throw your staff on the ground,” and it turned into a snake that chased him.
God said, “Pick it up by its tail,” and he did, and it became a staff again.

## 03.11 Moses objects again

*In the end, Moses won't go alone.*

Moses said that he could not speak well,
and God said he would give him the words.
Moses finally just asked God to send someone else,
but God is angry and tells him that his brother Aaron would speak Moses' words, but he had to go.
Moses obeyed and went back to Egypt to do what God had told him.

## 03.12 Facing Pharaoh

*Every time he asks — every time he's refused.*

Moses went to Pharaoh to negotiate the freedom of his people.
The Lord was with Moses.
Each time, Moses asked Pharaoh to let the people go away to worship their God.
Pharaoh refused,
and God brought a plague on the Egyptians.
Each time a plague came, Pharaoh asked Moses to pray to God to remove it,
and he would let the people go — but as soon as the plague was removed,
Pharaoh would harden his heart and refuse to let them go.

## 03.13 Ten plagues

*Each plague was a challenge to a different god.*

The Lord was with Moses.
There were 10 plagues — each represented a challenge to an Egyptian god.
For example, the Egyptians worshiped the god of the Nile,
and the first plague turned the river to blood.
They worshiped a god with a frog's head,
and they had a plague of frogs and other afflictions.

## 03.14 Passover: blood on the doorframes

*One night, one mark — between life and death.*

Moses left and went to the people with God's instructions.
Each household was to take a one-year-old spotless lamb,
sacrifice it,
and paint its blood with hyssop on the door frame.
That night the angel of the Lord came over.
The people of Israel who had put the blood on the door were saved,
while the firstborn son and animal of every other Egyptian household died.

## 03.15 Finally set free

*Night and day — God goes on ahead.*

Pharaoh told Moses to take his people and go.
They left Egypt.
The Lord sent a cloud by day
and fire by night to lead them across the land.
And he brought them to the edge of the Red Sea.

## 03.16 When Pharaoh changes his mind

*Freedom turns into danger.*

Pharaoh suddenly changed his mind
and came out with chariots to pursue the Hebrews,
who found themselves trapped between the Egyptian army and the Red Sea,
and they were very afraid.

## 03.17 When the sea opens

*Between fear and faith — what does Moses say?*

Moses told them, “Do not be afraid.
Stand firm, and you will see the deliverance the Lord will bring you today.
The Egyptians you see today you will never see again.
The Lord will fight for you; you need only to be still.”
God then acted to redeem his people.
Moses was told by God to put his rod in the water, and the waters of the sea divided, and the people walked through on dry land.

## 03.18 The end of the chase

*Those who crossed on dry ground — and those left behind.*

The Egyptians followed them in chariots,
and the wheels of their chariots got stuck.
The Egyptians tried to turn back
as they realised that the God of Israel was fighting for the Hebrews.
Once the people of God were through the sea, Moses raised his rod again,
and the Lord closed the waters, and the Egyptians were washed away.
The people of God simply had to stand and wait on God,
and then obey and walk through the sea.

## 03.19 Through the desert to Mount Sinai

*Bread from heaven, a covenant at the mountain's foot.*

After this, the people of God lived as nomads,
herding their sheep and walking towards the promised land.
God gave them manna to eat and water to drink.
Moses trusted in God and often acted as a mediator between God and the people.
Finally, the people came to the foot of Mount Sinai, and here God made a covenant with the people.
He had saved them and loves them, and now he gives them the Torah.

## 03.20 When the Torah was given

*Before the law, there was a rescue.*

The Torah is the working-out of God's faithfulness to his people, and through them to the world,
to fulfil the obligations of living under the covenant.
God said: “I AM the Lord who brought you out of slavery.”
Importantly, God delivered the people from Egypt and THEN gave them instructions.
God gave his covenant, before to Abraham and now to Moses.
In neither of these was acceptance conditional on obeying laws.
God redeemed them, and then gave the Torah.

## 03.21 The Ten Commandments

*With God, and with each other — what is the relationship built on?*

He gave them 10 simple commandments, beginning with God saying: Love God
— do not make any idols.
Do not take the name of the Lord your God in vain. Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. Honour your father and mother.
Do not murder. Do not commit adultery. Do not steal. Do not bear false witness. Do not covet.
These commands aim at restoring the relationship between God and his people,
and restoring their relationships with each other.
Think what the world could be like if we all obeyed these!

## 03.22 Civil laws

*A new nation — a new constitution.*

Later, more detail was given in 3 main forms.
Civil laws — to help them set up as a nation,
a constitution for this new nation.
Although these have great wisdom, they may not all apply to us today.
Many of these laws were given to help this nomadic people
live safely and healthily in the desert at that time.
But they also included care for the poor,
allowing refugees to live among them as if born in their country,
land justice, animal care, and more.

## 03.23 Holiness and the priesthood

*What do the outward signs actually mean?*

All of it reflects God's own character.
Ceremonial instructions were given pertaining to holiness — washing rituals to symbolise clean living.
These were external symbols of purity and forgiveness.
Priests and religious leaders were held to a higher standard.
These ceremonial laws were pointing forward to the priest who was to come, and who would be perfect.
The moral obligations were meant to restore relationship with God
and with people,
including how they cared for animals and the land.

## 03.24 The holy tent

*God chooses to live among His people.*

In addition to the Torah, the Lord made his presence known in another way.
He gave them specific instructions to make a special tent, a tabernacle.
They put up a tent to represent God living with his people.
People in their own tents would look out and see this tent,
a sign that God was with them.
They didn't need to be afraid, because God was with them.

## 03.25 The sacrificial system

*In place of guilt — a flawless animal.*

The Lord gave them instructions for sacrifice,
which involved a clean animal being provided to atone for the sins of the people.
Once a year, the high priest would take a goat without blemish for a sacrifice,
and sprinkle its blood on the people.
Their clothing carried the mark of this sacrifice for their sins.
A second goat would carry the sins of those far from the camp away into the wilderness.

## 03.26 What does this point to?

*The symbols point toward something — or Someone.*

These symbols are all pointing forward to the fulfilment of God's plan,
which would happen when the Messiah came to be the one true, perfect lamb,
enabling a real restoration of humanity in which we are transformed on the inside.

## 03.27 At the edge of the promised land

*Fear stretched it into 40 years.*

The Hebrew people were led through the desert
and were brought to the edge of the land that God had promised.
However, the Hebrews were afraid to go in,
and so wandered for 40 years as refugees.
Finally, Moses died, and God called Joshua to lead them into the land that God had promised.
Joshua means “He saves!”

## 03.28 This is our story too

*Through one man's life, death, and resurrection — a far greater plan.*

This is not just the narrative of how God saved his people from slavery in Egypt,
but it points to a much greater story — our story —
of God's unfolding plan to save all nations from slavery to evil, through to the life, death,
and resurrection of the one who was to come…
