# Life of Jesus (Part 2)

## 08.01 Hope for the outcast

*Who was Jesus' teaching really for?*

Jesus' preaching brought hope to the poor.
He included the outcast and the disabled, those who were rejected.
In doing so, Jesus made clear how far the religious leaders had drifted from what God actually wanted, which angered them.

## 08.02 A modern misunderstanding

*Some picture Him differently — but who's right?*

Some people today say that Christianity has become far too liberal and permissive.
This idea often comes from movies out of Hollywood and the West.
But that has nothing to do with Jesus!

## 08.03 “I have come to fulfill”

*Not to abolish the law — so what, instead?*

Jesus said, “Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets;
I have not come to abolish them, but to fulfill them.”

## 08.04 The law of the heart

*Outward behavior isn't enough — what matters?*

“You have heard that it was said to the people long ago, 'You shall not murder, and anyone who murders will be subject to judgment.'
But I tell you that anyone who is angry with a brother or sister will be subject to judgment.
You have heard that it was said, 'You shall not commit adultery.'
But I tell you that anyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart.
The law also says you must not break a sworn oath.”

## 08.05 “Yes” or “no” is enough

*No need for oaths — why not?*

But Jesus takes this further and simply teaches:
say 'Yes' or 'No.'
Anything beyond that, he says,
comes from the evil one.
We are simply to tell the truth!

## 08.06 What holiness means

*Loving your enemy — is it even possible?*

Jesus said that all the commandments are summed up in loving God with all your heart and mind, and loving your neighbor as yourself.
He also taught that we should love our enemies
and pray for those who persecute us.
He said, “Be holy, because your Father in heaven is holy.”

## 08.07 Do not judge

*Before removing the speck from another's eye — do what?*

Then Jesus says, “Do not judge, so that you will not be judged.
Do not point out the speck in your neighbor's eye without first taking the plank out of your own eye.”
This makes it clear to us: we are all guilty of breaking God's law.
Because he says, if we break even one command, we have broken them all.

## 08.08 We cannot save ourselves

*Who is good enough? The answer is surprising.*

So we cannot judge or condemn others, because in breaking God's commands, we too are guilty.
We cannot save ourselves.
We need God to save us
and forgive us.
None of us is good enough.

## 08.09 A woman caught

*The law is clear — but who carries it out?*

One day, to trap him, the religious teachers brought a woman who had been caught in adultery
and said, “According to the ancient law, this woman must be stoned.”
Jesus, without looking up from what he was writing on the ground,
said to them, “Let whoever among you is without sin be the first to throw a stone at her.”

## 08.10 “Let the sinless one throw first”

*One by one, they leave — why?*

When they heard this, the men, one by one, starting with the eldest, walked away without a word,
until no one was left but the woman.
Jesus asked her, “Has no one condemned you?”
She said to him, “No one.”
Jesus said to her, “Neither do I condemn you.
Go, and sin no more.”

## 08.11 The twelve are chosen

*A fisherman — a future leader.*

Jesus' disciple Peter recognized who he was.
At the beginning of his ministry, Jesus chose 12 men, calling them to be his disciples, and continued his ministry traveling with them among the people.
One of them, named Peter, was a fisherman.
Jesus called him to be the leader of the disciples.
One day Jesus asked them,

## 08.12 “Who do they say I am?”

*The question is asked — what was the answer?*

“Who do people say I am?”
— when he asked them,
some said he was John the Baptist,
others said Elijah, and still others, one of the ancient prophets.
Then Jesus asked them, “But who do you say I am?”
Peter said to him, “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God,”
he answered.

## 08.13 Jesus foretells his own end

*The disciples don't understand.*

Jesus told him that God himself had revealed this to him.
After that, Jesus told his disciples that he would go through great suffering,
be persecuted by the chief priests and teachers of the law,
be killed, and rise again on the third day.
But they did not fully understand what he was telling them.

## 08.14 Lazarus falls ill

*Jesus doesn't leave right away — why?*

Jesus had very close friends —
siblings named Mary, Martha, and Lazarus, who lived in Bethany, near Jerusalem.
While Jesus was traveling and teaching, Mary and Martha sent word to him that Lazarus was very ill.
But Jesus continued his ministry for two more days before setting out to see them.
Those following him were terrified, because it was very dangerous to go back to Bethany.
They said:

## 08.15 “Are you really going back there?”

*Danger lies ahead.*

“Rabbi,” they said,
“a short while ago the Jews there tried to stone you,
and yet you are going back?”
Then Jesus told them that his friend Lazarus had died.

## 08.16 Martha comes out to meet him

*Between faith and doubt.*

When he arrived, Jesus learned that Lazarus had already been in the tomb for several days.
Bethany was about two miles from Jerusalem, so many people had come to comfort Martha and Mary.
When Martha heard that Jesus was coming, she went out to meet him,
but Mary stayed at home.
Martha said to Jesus, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.
But I know that even now, whatever you ask, God will give you.”

## 08.17 “I am the resurrection and the life”

*One answer — for every fear.*

Jesus said to her, “Your brother will rise again.”
Martha replied, “I know he will rise again in the resurrection at the last day.”
Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life.
Whoever believes in me will live, even though they die, and whoever lives by believing in me will never die.
Do you believe this?”
“Yes, Lord,” she replied, “I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, who is to come into the world.”

## 08.18 Mary arrives

*Why does Jesus weep?*

Then Martha went and called her sister Mary aside and said, “The Teacher is here, and he's asking for you.”
When Mary heard this, she got up quickly, went to him, and fell at his feet,
saying, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.”
Seeing her weeping, and the Jews who had come with her also weeping, Jesus was deeply moved in spirit.
He asked, “Where have you laid him?”
“Come and see, Lord,” they answered.
When Jesus arrived at the tomb, he was deeply moved, and tears came to his eyes.

## 08.19 “Take away the stone”

*Martha objects.*

It was a cave with a stone laid across the entrance.
Jesus said, “Take away the stone.”
Martha objected that there would be a bad smell.
Jesus said to her, “Did I not tell you that if you believe, you will see the glory of God?”

## 08.20 “Lazarus, come out!”

*After the prayer — a single command.*

After they took away the stone, Jesus looked up and said,
“Father, I thank you that you have heard me.
I knew that you always hear me,
but I said this for the sake of the people standing here,
so that they may believe that you sent me.”
When he had said this, Jesus called out in a loud voice,
“Lazarus, come out!”

## 08.21 Alive out of the tomb

*A man wrapped in grave clothes — what does it mean?*

The dead man came out, his hands and feet wrapped in strips of linen,
and a cloth around his face.
Jesus said to them, “Take off his grave clothes and let him go.”
Many who had seen this went to the religious leaders and reported it.
So they began plotting how to kill Jesus, fearing that many would follow him.

## 08.22 Why did Jesus weep?

*This is more than just tears.*

So what was happening here? Jesus cried.
Why did he weep, if he was about to raise Lazarus?
The word in the original text is closer to howling.
He wasn't merely shedding tears — he was crying out loud, in anguish.
This wasn't how it was meant to be. But it shows his grief for all who suffer, or who lose their lives too soon.
God never wanted our world to be like this.

## 08.23 Moving forward

*This was his own choice.*

But even knowing this act would push the religious leaders further toward killing him, Jesus went ahead and raised Lazarus.
He chose to move forward, to set things right.
Jesus was moving forward toward his own death —
willingly giving his life, and rising again, so that we too might rise with him.
From this point on, death would no longer be the end of the story…
